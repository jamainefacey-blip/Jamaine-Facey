# VOYAGE SMART TRAVEL — WHOLE-PRODUCT BENCHMARK ROUNDTABLE AUDIT

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Status:** Founder-directed pre-build audit and product unification map  
**Scope:** Entire Voyage Smart Travel proposition — not accessibility alone

## 1. Roundtable mandate

Voyage Smart Travel must operate as one travel intelligence body across the full journey. It must benchmark, match or outperform category leaders without copying their fragmented product structures.

The benchmark covers:

- inspiration and destination discovery;
- flexible-date, nearby-airport and multi-city flight search;
- package holidays;
- backpacking and social travel;
- family and group travel;
- solo travel;
- luxury, VIP and concierge travel;
- yacht, cruise and specialist transport;
- rail, coach, bus, taxi, ride-hail, car hire and airport transfers;
- hotels, villas, hostels and alternative accommodation;
- activities, attractions and local experiences;
- safety, disruption, insurance and recovery;
- accessibility and neuro-inclusive travel;
- affiliate comparison and browser-first handoff;
- live journey support before, during and after travel.

## 3. Consensus finding

VST already contains useful modules, pages, APIs, schemas and data, but the product is fragmented by travel mode and implementation phase. The principal gap is not a lack of ideas. It is the absence of one canonical journey model, one traveller profile, one comparison method, one evidence system and one live companion spanning every travel class.

VST must not become a collection of separate mini-sites for flights, hotels, accessibility, VIP, backpacking, yachts and transfers. Every capability must plug into one journey graph.

## 4. One-body operating model

Every trip must use the same canonical chain:

`Traveller intent → Traveller profile → Journey requirements → Search matrix → Option normalisation → Suitability/risk/value scoring → Explainable recommendation → Affiliate/provider handoff → Confirmation tracking → Live journey companion → Disruption recovery → Post-trip learning`

### Shared objects

Every travel mode must reuse:

- one Traveller Profile;
- one Accessibility Passport;
- one Trip Brief;
- one Journey Graph;
- one Provider Record;
- one Offer Record;
- one Evidence Record;
- one Risk and Suitability Score;
- one Booking/Handoff Record;
- one Live Trip State;
- one Incident/Recovery Record;
- one Learning Record.

No vertical may create a separate customer identity, scoring system, booking state, partner registry or alert engine.

## 6. Evidence-backed external benchmark observations

### Fare and route optimisation

A competitive fare engine must include whole-month or flexible-date views, nearby airports, broad destination discovery, price alerts and multi-city journeys. VST must go further by calculating the true journey total and risk rather than presenting the headline fare alone.

### Package and assisted travel

Leading package providers combine assisted-travel teams, airport support, accessible accommodation, accessible transfers, neuro-inclusive services and in-destination help. VST should aggregate and compare those provider capabilities rather than duplicate the operational assistance itself.

### Backpacking and social travel

Leading backpacker platforms connect travellers through city/hostel chats, profiles, events and travel plans. VST must add safety controls, accessibility, route intelligence, budget control and browser-accessible alternatives rather than forcing all value into an app-only community.

### Luxury and VIP

Luxury leaders provide pre-arrival concierge, individually created itineraries, private drivers, premium dining and continuous personal service. VST should translate that level of coordination into a scalable preference-and-orchestration engine, with human concierge escalation where contracted.

### Yacht charter

Charter leaders separate bareboat and crewed products, verify experience, support owner/guest messaging, explain booking acceptance and expose hidden-cost questions. VST must normalise base charter, crew, fuel, food, berthing, tax, gratuity, deposit, transfer and itinerary feasibility into one true-cost view.

### Ground transport

Ride-hail leaders distinguish wheelchair-accessible vehicles and assisted standard vehicles, with availability varying by location. VST must never display a generic accessible-transfer label without vehicle, location, availability and confirmation evidence.

## 7. Critical gaps overlooked

### P0 — Product truth and trust

1. Static demonstration hotels, accessibility scores and certifications can be mistaken for verified live inventory.
2. Several strong customer-facing claims exceed the current evidence and deployment state.
3. There is no universal provider evidence record with source, date, route/location scope and expiry.
4. There is no single classification separating DEMO, SPEC, PARTIAL, VERIFIED and LIVE throughout the website.
5. Affiliate ranking neutrality is canonical but not yet technically enforced.

### P0 — One journey body

1. Flight, hotel, accessibility, safety, booking and AVA features are not proven to share one journey object end to end.
2. There is no universal offer-normalisation model across packages, hostels, yachts, taxis, rail and activities.
3. There is no true-total-cost engine across baggage, transfers, deposits, resort fees, fuel, tips, insurance and positioning travel.
4. There is no universal disruption graph showing which downstream bookings are affected.
5. There is no confirmation tracker for assistance requests and provider promises.

### P1 — Search depth

1. Flexible date matrices, nearby-country positioning and stopover optimisation are not proven live.
2. Self-transfer and separate-ticket risk are not consistently scored.
3. Ground transport and final-mile cost are not included in every itinerary.
4. Backpacking, cruise and yacht journeys lack production-grade search models.
5. VIP and low-cost travel are treated as categories rather than profiles using the same core engine.

### P1 — Traveller modes

VST needs configurable journey modes, not separate products:

- Lowest Cost
- Best Overall Value
- Fastest
- Lowest Stress
- Accessibility First
- Family
- Solo Safety
- Backpacker
- Digital Nomad
- Luxury/VIP
- Adventure
- Eco
- Medical Travel
- Group

Each mode changes weights in the same scoring engine; it must not fork the platform.

### P1 — Service recovery

1. No proven automatic re-plan when one flight, transfer or hotel fails.
2. No rights/compensation guidance linked to actual itinerary events.
3. No evidence that AVA can contact or guide the user through each provider handoff.
4. No unified incident timeline for claims, receipts, conversations and resolutions.

### P2 — Commercial completeness

1. Affiliate coverage is not mapped by vertical, geography, commission, conversion quality and data access.
2. No commission-blind ranking test exists.
3. No browser-link quality check exists to prevent app-only or broken handoffs.
4. No abandoned-booking recovery or price-change explanation standard exists.
5. No margin model exists for optional premium concierge or partner service fees.

## 8. Canonical scoring model

Every normalised itinerary must produce transparent component scores:

- Total Cost
- Time
- Convenience
- Reliability
- Safety
- Accessibility
- Comfort
- Flexibility
- Experience Value
- Environmental Impact
- Evidence Confidence
- Provider Support

The customer chooses priorities directly or through a travel mode. The recommendation must show:

- why it ranked first;
- what was sacrificed;
- the difference from the cheapest option;
- all material extras;
- evidence quality;
- confirmations still required;
- affiliate disclosure.

## 10. Recommended implementation sequence

### Stage 1 — Truth and unification

- inventory all existing VST pages, data, APIs, schemas and engines;
- map each to the canonical journey chain;
- label every capability and claim;
- remove or clearly mark unsupported live claims;
- create one provider, offer and evidence schema.

### Stage 2 — Universal comparison engine

- normalise flights, packages, hotels, hostels, transfers, rail, coach, car hire, activities, cruises and yachts;
- create true-total-cost calculation;
- create traveller-mode weighting;
- create explainable scoring.

### Stage 3 — Website experience

- one conversational trip intake;
- one visual journey builder;
- mode switching without restarting;
- side-by-side cheapest, recommended and lowest-stress options;
- evidence and assistance panels;
- browser-first partner handoff.

### Stage 4 — Live journey and recovery

- confirmation tracking;
- provider status monitoring;
- disruption dependency graph;
- automatic alternatives;
- rights, claims and receipt vault;
- AVA live companion.

### Stage 5 — Specialist depth

- backpacker social/safety layer;
- VIP concierge layer;
- yacht/cruise charter layer;
- medical and complex-access travel;
- group and corporate travel.

## 11. Roundtable classifications

### VERIFIED

- VST already contains accessibility pages, filters, static data, user-profile specifications, AVA endpoints, fare-search components, safety pages and booking structures.
- VST has a canonical Vercel project and GitHub source.
- Accessibility Intelligence is now recorded as founder-binding canon on PR #17.

### LIKELY

- Reusing the existing modules under one journey model will be faster and safer than rebuilding the website by vertical.
- VST can differentiate through total-journey suitability, evidence and recovery rather than inventory volume alone.
- Affiliate breadth will be commercially stronger when ranking remains demonstrably commission-neutral.

### ASSUMPTION — MUST BE PROVEN

- Existing accessibility scores and certifications correspond to real verified providers.
- Current fare, hotel and itinerary engines are live and production-complete.
- Existing modules share persistent user, trip and booking state.
- VST can currently complete provider assistance requests or automatic disruption recovery.

## 12. Consensus decision

Do not commence a broad visual website rebuild yet.

First complete the Stage 1 truth-and-unification audit and the ten build-readiness artefacts. Then build the website around one canonical journey body. Existing modules should be recovered, tested and connected—not replaced by a new collection of disconnected features.

**Roundtable confidence:** 94%
