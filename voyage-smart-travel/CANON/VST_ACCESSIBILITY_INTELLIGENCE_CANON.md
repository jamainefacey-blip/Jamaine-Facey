# VOYAGE SMART TRAVEL ACCESSIBILITY INTELLIGENCE CANON

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Authority:** Founder binding canon  
**Founder:** Jamaine Facey  
**Status:** Mandatory for Voyage Smart Travel product, research, affiliate selection, itinerary generation, UI, data, content and partner workflows

## 1. Founding position

Voyage Smart Travel does not treat accessibility as a filter, add-on, compliance page or special-case service.

VST is an **AI Accessibility Concierge** and accessibility intelligence layer that sits above airlines, airports, hotels, holiday providers, transfer companies, attractions and affiliate partners.

The traveller explains their needs once. VST must then reduce repeated disclosure, avoidable stress, uncertainty and manual coordination across the full journey.

## 2. Affiliate operating model

VST complements and works alongside affiliate and travel partners rather than pretending to replace their operational services.

VST must:

- research and record the verified assistance each partner provides;
- distinguish published policy from live operational evidence and traveller reports;
- compare providers on suitability, not only price;
- direct travellers to the correct browser-accessible partner booking or assistance page;
- clearly disclose when an assistance request must be confirmed directly by the provider;
- never claim that VST itself supplies airport, airline, hotel or medical assistance unless that service is genuinely contracted and verified.

## 3. Accessibility Passport

VST must support one reusable, consent-based Accessibility Passport covering only information required for the journey.

The passport may include:

- mobility and transfer needs;
- wheelchair or mobility-equipment specifications;
- autism and neurodivergence needs;
- sensory triggers and calming preferences;
- hidden disabilities;
- visual or hearing access needs;
- communication preferences;
- queueing, boarding and seating needs;
- medication, medical equipment and refrigeration needs;
- dietary and allergy requirements;
- fatigue, pain and rest requirements;
- assistance-animal requirements;
- emergency contacts and chosen support people;
- preferred level of explanation and journey guidance.

The passport must be editable, exportable, privacy-first and shared only with explicit user authority.

## 4. AI Accessibility Concierge

For every relevant journey, VST should assess and explain:

- airline and holiday-provider support;
- departure, connection and arrival airport support;
- direct versus connecting-flight burden;
- walking distance, terminal changes and transfer complexity;
- boarding process and likely queue exposure;
- aircraft and seat suitability where evidence exists;
- hotel entrance, room, bathroom, lift and route accessibility;
- accessible transfers and vehicle suitability;
- sensory load, noise, crowding and disruption risk;
- accessible attractions, beaches, restaurants and local transport;
- nearby pharmacies, hospitals and emergency support;
- the evidence source, date checked and any confirmation still required.

The best itinerary is the one that provides the best overall balance of suitability, safety, stress, time and cost—not automatically the cheapest itinerary.

## 5. Accessibility Confidence Score

Every accessible itinerary should be capable of receiving an Accessibility Confidence Score backed by transparent components.

Possible components include:

- airline support confidence;
- airport support confidence;
- connection complexity;
- mobility burden;
- sensory burden;
- queue and boarding burden;
- hotel accessibility evidence;
- transfer accessibility evidence;
- medical and dietary support;
- disruption and recovery resilience;
- recency and quality of supporting evidence;
- number of provider confirmations still outstanding.

The score must never imply a guarantee. It must show why the score was awarded, what is verified, what is unverified and what the traveller should confirm.

## 6. Evidence classification

Accessibility claims must be classified as:

- **VERIFIED** — current official provider information or direct operational confirmation;
- **SUPPORTED** — credible third-party evidence consistent with provider information;
- **REPORTED** — traveller experience that may vary by route, location, staff or date;
- **UNVERIFIED** — not yet supported sufficiently for a recommendation;
- **OUTDATED** — no longer safe to rely on without rechecking.

VST must record the source and date checked. Material claims must be rechecked before a booking recommendation is presented.

## 7. Journey design rules

VST must:

- prefer fewer risky handoffs and less physical or cognitive burden;
- show the cost difference between the cheapest and most suitable itinerary;
- identify when a stopover creates a useful rest experience versus added risk;
- avoid self-transfers unless the traveller knowingly accepts the risk;
- build recovery time around separate tickets and assistance handoffs;
- explain airport, airline, hotel and transfer responsibilities separately;
- include browser-accessible links rather than app-only or forced-download links;
- provide printable, mobile, audio-friendly and plain-language journey versions;
- support interruption recovery so the traveller does not need to repeat their needs.

## 8. Neuro-inclusive travel

For autistic, ADHD, anxious, cognitively disabled or sensory-sensitive travellers, VST should support:

- visual journey previews and step-by-step expectations;
- quiet-route and quiet-time recommendations;
- sensory-room and hidden-disability scheme information;
- reduced-queue and early-boarding options;
- predictable meal, seating and transfer plans;
- disruption warnings and alternative-plan preparation;
- communication cards and assistance-request summaries;
- accommodation requests concerning noise, lighting, room position and routine.

No diagnosis should be required where a functional need can be stated directly.

## 9. Disabled-user-first product law

All VST interfaces and outputs must be usable through supported combinations of voice, keyboard, screen reader, magnification, switch, pointer and eye-gaze interaction.

Accessibility Intelligence is not complete if the traveller must:

- repeatedly re-enter the same needs;
- understand airline or airport jargon;
- search multiple provider pages unaided;
- compare incompatible assistance policies manually;
- use an app-only link when a browser route exists;
- interpret an unexplained score;
- discover critical restrictions only after booking.

## 10. Partner benchmark obligation

VST must maintain a living partner accessibility benchmark covering major airlines, airports, hotels, package-holiday providers, cruise operators, transfer providers and affiliate platforms.

The benchmark should record:

- assistance offered;
- neuro-inclusion support;
- mobility-equipment policy;
- booking and notice deadlines;
- companion or carer policies;
- seating and boarding support;
- hotel accessibility evidence quality;
- contact methods and response channels;
- known route or location variation;
- affiliate availability;
- browser booking and assistance links;
- date last verified.

VST should aim to outperform partners by making their fragmented services easier to discover, compare, coordinate and understand.

## 11. Commercial and trust boundary

VST may earn affiliate revenue, but suitability and safety must not be ranked below commission.

Recommendations must disclose affiliate relationships where required and must never manipulate an accessibility recommendation to favour a higher-paying partner.

A partner should not receive a strong accessibility rating merely because it publishes a policy. The evidence must support the actual journey being recommended.

## 12. Product pillars

Accessibility Intelligence is a core VST pillar alongside:

1. AI Travel Planning;
2. Safety and Risk Intelligence;
3. Accessibility Intelligence;
4. Smart Savings and Route Optimisation;
5. Live Journey Companion.

## 13. Binding product question

Every VST feature and recommendation must answer:

> Does this reduce stress, increase independence, improve safety or expand meaningful travel choice for the traveller?

If not, the feature or recommendation must be reconsidered.

## 14. Current implementation status

This canon records product authority and design direction. It does not by itself prove that the Accessibility Passport, Concierge, Confidence Score or partner benchmark are implemented.

Each capability must remain classified as PLANNED, PARTIAL, VERIFIED or LIVE according to behavioural and runtime evidence.
